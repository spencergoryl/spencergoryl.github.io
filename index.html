Preface: the concepts behind this collection are hard for me to put into words. That’s why my thoughts appear last.

Engram

An engram is the physical trace left in the brain by a memory. I liked this word because it describes something I have been thinking about for a long time. Why do certain experiences seem to leave something behind? Something that changes the way we experience things afterward.

I'm fascinated how a simple experience can burn itself into your memory in a way that most things just don't. A relaxing bike ride on a warm night, the humidity and the certain smell of the air. For whatever reason that feeling stays with you and impacts your perception of all the nighttime bike rides in the future. Perhaps you watch a movie at the right time in your life and years later you realize it has somehow become part of your identity. You hear a song and it brings back a version of yourself that you haven't thought about in years.

I imagine the types of things that trigger this reaction can say a lot about a person. However, the part that interests me is trying to capture the feeling of a memory that has become part of how you perceive the world. The scenes aren't meant to recreate specific memories from my life. They represent the lens that a deeply impactful memory can put over other experiences.

The visual style that I developed for this collection grew from how I have learned to render the human figure. On skin, a subtle blend can make just as much impact as a sharp edge. I wanted to approach the environments with the same balance of soft and defined. Some parts of the scene are simple while other parts are very precise, but I tried to make it all as intentional as the subtle rendering of a human face.

I wanted to see if I could make an image that had the same kind of staying power as the things that inspired me. Something that demands to be remembered.
